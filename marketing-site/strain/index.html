Push hard. Know when to back off.

Muscles adapt in weeks. Tendons take longer. Calisthenics Tree moves you up when you've earned it, drops you down when a set falls apart, and warns you when a joint is taking more load than it's used to.

Three rules, no guesswork

1. Unlock: hit every target set

Each node has a target, like 3 × 12 s tuck front lever hold or 3 × 5 pull-ups. Log all the target sets at the target reps or hold time and the next node in the tree unlocks. No streaks, no points to grind.

2. Regress: when a set falls apart

Every node also has a critical-fail line below the target. If a set lands under it, say a tuck front lever held for less than 8 seconds, and the node has a regression, the app moves you to that easier node straight away. Where there's more than one regression, it picks the preferred one first.

Each one is a deliberate choice: a failed freestanding handstand drops to the wall handstand, a failed toes-to-bar drops to the L-sit. See every branch.

3. Ease off: the strain score

Every set you log adds load to the joints that move works. The score per set is:

intensity × reps or seconds × rank

Most moves have an intensity of 1.0. Straight-arm skills like the front lever, freestanding handstand and planche push-up count 1.8×, because they load tendons hard at long lever lengths. Higher legs ranks count up to 2.4×.

Load is tracked separately for seven joint pathways: bent-arm elbow, bent-arm shoulder, straight-arm elbow, straight-arm shoulder, wrist, lower back and hip flexors. Each set counts toward every pathway its move uses.

How the warning works

The app compares each pathway's load this week with its average over the previous three weeks:

  • OK

    At or near your recent average.

  • Watch

    More than 1.15× your average. Trending up; keep an eye on it.

  • Deload

    More than 1.5× your average. Take an easier session or train a regression.

You'll find it under Insights, with a four-week sparkline for each pathway. With no history yet there's nothing to compare against, so the first weeks set your baseline.

Worked example: tuck front lever (rank 6)
One set: 1.8 × 12 s × rank 6129.6
Three sets, one session388.8
Two sessions this week (straight-arm shoulder)777.6
Previous three weeks, average480
Ratio: 777.6 ÷ 4801.62× · Deload

Illustrative numbers. Your score depends on what you log.

Not medical advice

The strain score is a training-load guide built from what you log. It can't see pain, sleep, old injuries or anything outside the app, and it isn't a diagnosis.

If something hurts, stop, and talk to a doctor or physiotherapist before you train through it.

Start where you are

A short placement puts you on the right rung of all four trees, so the strain score starts from honest training, not rank-one busywork.
