How it works

No program PDF to decode. Train one node at a time, and let the tree decide what's next.

  1. Find your rung

    Answer two yes/no questions and do one set of push-ups. You start each tree where you actually are, which isn't always rank one.

  2. Train today's node

    Every node has a clear target, like 3 × 12 s tuck front lever. The built-in timer and rep counter keep score while you work.

  3. Earn the next one

    Hit every target set and the next node unlocks. Fall well short and the app drops you to a smarter regression instead of letting you grind.

Questions, answered

Is it for beginners?

Yes. Every tree starts at an entry move: wall push-up, dead hang, plank and bodyweight squat. Placement starts you wherever you actually are.

Do I need equipment?

Not to start. The placement test is a set of push-ups, and the Push, Core and Legs trees begin on the floor. The Pull tree needs a pull-up bar from rank 1.

Later ranks use parallel bars or parallettes (L-sit), rings (planche push-up), a box (assisted pistol) and a light weight (weighted pistol). See every rank.

How does placement work?

Two yes/no questions (can you do a clean pull-up, then can you hold a 15-second support or a 10-second active hang) sort you into one of four starting points.

Then you do one set of push-ups, stopping with about two reps left in the tank. Fewer than 5 reps moves you back two rungs, 5 to 12 keeps you there, more than 12 moves you up one.

What's the strain score?

Every logged set adds load to the joints it works: intensity × reps or seconds × the skill's rank. Straight-arm skills count 1.8×. When a joint's load this week passes 1.5× its average over the previous three weeks, the app warns you to take an easier session or pick a regression. Read the full breakdown.

Is it medical advice?

No. The strain score is a training-load guide based on what you log, not a diagnosis. If something hurts, stop and see a qualified health professional.

Is there an App Store or Google Play app?

Not yet. Calisthenics Tree runs in your browser on phone or desktop, and you can add it to your home screen.
